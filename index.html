Understanding the three curves

What is a streamline?

Take a photo of a flow at one particular instant. The direction of the velocity in that photo, drawn as curves, gives the streamlines.

At time t₁ the velocity direction looked like some set of curves, and at time t₂ it looked like some other set. This does not mean a streamline is directly a function of time. A streamline is determined by the velocity field. If the velocity field changes with time, the streamlines change with time too. In steady flow the velocity field doesn’t change, so the streamlines are constant curves over time.

A streamline is a curve whose tangent at every point is along the velocity direction at that instant. One streamline passes through every point, so a single photo contains a whole family of streamlines, not just one curve.

What is a pathline?

Take a flowing fluid and pick one drop or water particle. Follow it. The path it travels, the line it moves along, is the pathline.

Once the particle is chosen (release point and release time fixed), its pathline is one fixed curve. Changing the observation time doesn’t change the curve, it only extends further as the particle moves on. In unsteady flow a streamline is different: it changes at every instant.

The pathline of particle P is given by x(t), y(t), which tells us where the particle went as time passed.

What is a streakline?

The velocity of the flow changes with time. So the particle released just behind the one we followed for the pathline need not follow the same path. For example, if the velocity varies sinusoidally, the particles behind it will not follow the first one. Each particle follows a somewhat different path.

To get a streakline, place a dye injector at a fixed point A that continuously releases particles. At time t₂, take a snapshot and join the current positions of all particles that passed through A at any time before t₂. The joined line is the streakline of point A at time t₂.

The oldest particle has travelled farthest, and the newest particle is still at A. We join the particles in order of their release time.

So a streakline changes with the observation time and also with the reference point we choose. It tells us what curve forms if all the particles released from one point are joined at a given time.

Steady vs unsteady flow

In steady flow, streamlines, pathlines and streaklines coincide. In unsteady flow, they can be different.

Steady aur unsteady flow mein difference

Steady flow mein streamlines, pathlines aur streaklines ek hi hoti hain. Lekin unsteady flow mein ye teeno alag-alag ho sakti hain.
